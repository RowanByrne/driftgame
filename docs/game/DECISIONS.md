# Decisions

The user's decisions about this game, so they aren't reopened later. Before proposing something, check it hasn't already been decided here.

- **One line each, newest at the bottom:** the date, the decision, and why. Quote the user's own words when they say it best.
- **Phase gates go here too:** when the user says go at a gate (docs/workflow/WORKFLOW.md).
- **Never delete a line.** When a decision changes, add a new line and mark the old one "superseded by <date>".
- **Detail lives elsewhere.** A decision that changes a design or contract also updates that doc (`GDD.md`, `CONTRACTS.md`, a feature spec); this line just records that it was decided and why.

| 2026-11-04 | Gravity acts on the car while it is on the ground, along the surface, as the reference does it. One number, `Config.Drift.GravityOnGround`; 0 gives the old model back exactly. **The first change to the drift model since the feel was approved** | The user asked for a downhill road and said what it should feel like: "when i slide over a crest and into a downhill drift, im sliding faster and not slowing down as much as i would if it was flat". The decompile answered it: the reference never turns gravity off, and four suspension springs cancel only the part pointing into the surface, so the car runs away downhill with no code that mentions slopes. Ours is kinematic and needs the spring's result rather than the spring. Measured: on a 9.3 degree slope, 4 seconds from 40 studs/s, the car gains 20.5 studs/s downhill and loses 21.0 uphill, where before it did neither. On the flat the term is exactly zero and every suite reproduces its old numbers step for step (`features/road-elevation.md`, `features/drift-model.md`) |
| 2026-11-06 | The touge's banked corner ships at **10 degrees of bank**, not the 30 the spec asks for. The real fix is a car that can unstick itself from a wall (`T-074`); with that the bank goes to 30 (`features/road-elevation.md`) |
| 2026-11-08 | The tyre sound is **on all the time while the car is grounded and moving**, idling quietly and swelling into the squeal as the slip opens, the way the reference does it, and its peak drops to about half of what it was. **The clip itself stays the 3-second `124978127809544`** — three longer Creator Store candidates were played and the user kept the one in the game | The user said the squeal "isn't great. | 2026-11-08 | The always-on tyre hiss is **rejected after one drive**: the squeal is silent again unless a tyre is actually sliding. Its volume is now the **lateral slide speed** (`speed × sin(slip)`), not the slip angle, and its peak drops from 0.468 to 0.340 through the master | The user drove the always-on version the same afternoon they approved it: *"much better, but still could be better. the permanent squeel doesn't work for me. do it like drifto, but it should be inaudible when you start driving. if no tires are drifting, no squeal should be heard. It's to loud still generally. it's volume should be based off how hard it's actually sliding and/or drifting."* The third sentence is the real change and it is **not** what the reference does — the reference drives the squeal off the angle alone, so a car pivoting at walking pace screams as loudly as one flat out. Slide speed is both "how fast" and "how sideways" at once, which is what a real tyre's noise follows. Measured: 15° at 40 studs/s drops about 8 dB and a 90° pivot at 10 studs/s about 13.6 dB, while a genuine fast slide is only 2.8 dB down. `RollVolume` is kept at 0 so the always-on version is one number away. **The clip still never stops** — silence is a volume, not a stop, so its loop seam is still never re-heard (`AUDIO.md`, `features/car-fx.md`) |
